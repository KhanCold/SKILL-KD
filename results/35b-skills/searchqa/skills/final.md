[RULE 001] Definition Before Subtype
content: When a short-answer snippet question offers both a broad category and a more specific subtype, named entity, or symbolic example, do answer the broad category when the clue wording mentions a foreign-language or quoted common term that snippets define as that category or when the specific candidate is not a true subtype of the requested target type, and otherwise answer the directly supported more specific subtype.
why: The failed rollouts overapplied subtype preference to choose a specific national symbol despite snippets defining the foreign common term as the broad category, while the teacher answered the broad translation; prior subtype cases still require the true supported subtype when no definition/translation boundary is present.

[RULE 003] Containing Target Entity
content: When a short-answer clue asks for “this” target type but gives facts about an associated entity or component belonging to, within, or at that target, including a title character within a work, do answer the containing named entity matching the target type rather than the associated entity, component, role, or description.
why: The failed rollout answered the title character's translated role even after an original-title wording retry, while the teacher answered the named film; the prior rule handled components within a target but did not explicitly cover title-character clues for works.

[RULE 005] Biographical Title Wordplay
content: When a terse short-answer clue pairs a target type with a quoted phrase and the surrounding source, category, or snippets frame the phrase as a biographical or self-descriptive title, do answer the target-type entity indicated by title wordplay or name echo rather than an entity merely associated with a work or topic bearing the quoted phrase.
why: The failed rollout treated the quoted phrase as a literal film title and chose an actress associated with it, while the succeeding rollout used the life-story framing and name echo to choose the comedienne whose name fits the title wordplay.

[RULE 006] Single Geographic Term Location
content: When a short-answer prompt is only a geographic name and snippets identify it within nested locations, do answer the state- or province-level location explicitly paired with it when available, otherwise answer the country-level location explicitly paired with it, rather than a county-level area, full repeated place name, or an entity-type phrase.
why: The failed rollout answered an entity-type phrase containing the country, while the teacher answered the country alone; the prior rule handled state/province over county for bare geographic names but did not specify a country fallback when no state- or province-level disambiguator is given.

[RULE 007] Offset Level Definition
content: When a bare descriptive definition hinges on an offset or staggered level relation and snippets include both a numerically similar repeated comparison topic and a specialized compound term tied directly to that offset relation, do answer the specialized compound term alone rather than the repeated comparison topic or that term plus an added generic target noun.
why: The failed rollout chose the frequent one-and-a-half comparison topic even after a broader definition-over-frequency retry, while the teacher identified the offset-layout term but added a generic noun; the sharpened rule makes the offset relation and answer brevity explicit.

[RULE 010] Contextual Surface Form
content: When a short-answer clue supports multiple surface forms for the same answer, do answer a common-language alias containing the clue's target-type noun over another-language alias when such aliases appear, otherwise answer the directly source-supplied or shortest context-disambiguated form without adding inferred qualifiers, expansions, inflections, appended names, initials, suffixes, descriptors, or generic type words.
why: The failed rollouts chose the alternate-language alias even after a buried alias-clause retry, while the expected answer was the common-language alias containing the clue's target noun; the previous surface-form rule emphasized shortening but did not make this alias priority salient.

[RULE 011] Derivative Source Entity
content: When a short-answer prompt gives a generic class plus a named entity and snippets describe that named entity as a tribute, cover, homage, imitation, parody, or themed version of another named entity, do answer the represented source named entity alone rather than the derivative item, its class, or a relationship-appended phrase.
why: The failed rollouts, including retries that buried this idea inside broader rules, answered the relationship phrase for a tribute act, while the teacher answered only the source group being represented.

[RULE 012] Process Step Substance Match
content: When a short-answer clue asks for a substance used in a process step and snippets mention multiple plausible substances, do answer the substance explicitly tied to the same step and timing described by the clue rather than a substance matching only an adjective, a neighboring step, or a general alternative.
why: The failed rollout first chose a caustic non-acid and then a less directly supported alternative acid, while the teacher chose the acid explicitly tied to the pre-process cleaning step in the snippets.

[RULE 013] Title or Corrected Phrase
content: When a non-bare short-answer clue is reproduced or nearly reproduced in a search-result snippet, do answer the directly supplied missing, corrected, intended, or unswapped wording of an unfinished, altered, misquoted, or sound-swapped phrase when snippets present that wording as what the clue phrase should be, rather than the result-title headword, named phenomenon, source title, author, speaker, or containing work; otherwise, if the clue states or implies a target type and a result title is a concise candidate answer or headword matching that target type, answer the title headword exactly without appending an implied generic target-type word rather than infer from loosely related neighboring snippets.
why: The failed rollouts kept answering the named linguistic phenomenon for an altered phrase despite snippets directly giving the intended counterpart, while the teacher answered that corrected wording; the prior quote-gap rule covered unfinished phrases but not transformed or unswapped phrase corrections, and a separate retry phrasing was not salient enough against the title-headword temptation.

[RULE 014] Bare Prompt Context
content: When a short-answer prompt is only one or more names, titles, catchphrases, or a standalone quoted line, do answer the repeatedly supported jurisdiction, organization, or affiliated named entity for a person-name prompt with an office, rank, or role title when snippets identify that entity as what the role governs or represents; otherwise, for a bare catchphrase or slogan whose snippets define it as used for a named campaign, ticket, or promotion, answer the principal named beneficiary alone rather than the phrase type, event context, or accompanying secondary names; otherwise answer the credited creator or speaker when snippets predominantly credit the exact wording to one, otherwise answer the broader named place, organization, work, source series, or title context when snippets predominantly attach the prompt terms to that shared context beyond a mere repetition or minimal title variant, and for a delimiter- or conjunction-separated list whose snippets merely repeat or index the entries without another clue answer the first listed entry rather than a relationship, associated individual, shortened name, or broad category.
why: The failed rollout answered the phrase type and the teacher included a secondary ticket name, while the expected answer was only the principal candidate repeatedly identified as using the slogan; the prior bare-prompt rule lacked a campaign-slogan beneficiary case.
